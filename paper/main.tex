\begin{abstract}
Web search has become a load-bearing capability of vision--language agents,
yet existing visual question answering (VQA) benchmarks cannot measure it
faithfully: static benchmarks leak into training corpora within months, and
end-to-end accuracy conflates three distinct failure sources---retrieving the
wrong evidence, being distracted by superfluous retrieved content, and
failing to exploit correctly retrieved evidence.
We present \bench, a fully automated benchmark that regenerates itself every
day from news published within the preceding 48 hours, making it
contamination-free \emph{by construction}: no model checkpoint can contain
the answers.
Each item couples a news image with a four-way multiple-choice question, a
gold answer, a verbatim evidence snippet, the source URL, and crawl
timestamps.
A three-stage construction pipeline---VLM question generation, a closed-book
anti-memorization filter, and an oracle answerability filter---guarantees
that every retained question is unanswerable without search yet answerable
with the gold evidence.
On top of the data we define a three-way diagnostic protocol
(\emph{closed-book} / \emph{with-search} / \emph{oracle-evidence}) whose
score gaps, combined with the agent's retrieval trace, decompose end-to-end
error into \emph{retrieval}, \emph{distraction}, and \emph{utilization}
failures.
A pilot build of 200 questions from 126 articles across 7 news sources shows
the necessity of our filters: 58\% of fluent, well-formed generated questions
were answerable by a strong VLM without any search and had to be discarded.
We release the pipeline, daily splits, and a public leaderboard.

\section{Introduction}

Multimodal agents increasingly answer questions by \emph{searching the web}:
recognizing what is depicted, formulating queries, reading retrieved pages,
and grounding an answer in fresh evidence. This loop is now central to
consumer assistants and agentic frameworks alike, but the community lacks a
way to measure it. The difficulty is twofold.

\paragraph{Contamination.}
Any static benchmark built from web content is eventually absorbed into
pretraining corpora; reported gains then conflate memorization with genuine
search competence. Periodically ``refreshed'' benchmarks mitigate but do not
eliminate the problem, because refresh cycles (months) are far slower than
crawl-to-train cycles of frontier labs.

\paragraph{Attribution.}
A single end-to-end accuracy number cannot tell a developer \emph{where} an
agent fails. The failure may occur at \emph{retrieval} (the crawl never
surfaces the evidence---imprecise queries, incomplete coverage), at
\emph{robustness} (the evidence is retrieved but drowned in irrelevant
context that misleads the model), or at \emph{utilization} (the model cannot
exploit evidence that is right in front of it). These call for entirely
different fixes---better query planning, better re-ranking and context
management, and better grounding, respectively.

We address both problems with \bench, a benchmark that is rebuilt from
scratch every day. Freshly published news (48-hour window) is crawled from
20+ RSS channels; a VLM converts each (image, article) pair into
image-grounded multiple-choice questions; and two self-verifying filters
enforce the defining property of the benchmark: a \emph{closed-book filter}
discards any question a strong VLM can answer without search (these
questions, 58\% of fluent candidates in our pilot, are precisely the
contamination that plagues static benchmarks), and an \emph{oracle filter}
discards any question the same VLM cannot answer even when handed the gold
evidence (malformed or under-specified items). Every retained item therefore
satisfies: \emph{unanswerable without search, answerable with the gold
evidence}.

Because each item carries its gold evidence and source URL, we can evaluate
any agent under three settings: closed-book (lower bound, $\approx$ chance by
construction), with-search (the headline score), and oracle-evidence (upper
bound). Combined with the agent's retrieval trace, the gaps between the three
scores decompose end-to-end error into retrieval, distraction, and
utilization failures (\S\ref{sec:protocol}). A distraction stress test
further injects $k$ topically related but irrelevant passages to chart
robustness as a function of retrieval noise.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \bench, to our knowledge the first \emph{daily} self-refreshing VQA
  benchmark, contamination-free by construction, with a fully automated
  pipeline costing $\sim$\$2/day and no human in the loop;
  \item a three-way diagnostic protocol that attributes agent error to
  retrieval, distraction, or utilization, aligned with the actionable levers
  of agent development;
  \item a longitudinal resource: daily splits accumulate into a time series
  that supports novel analyses (accuracy vs.\ event recency, decay of
  ``liveness'' as events enter training data);

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Knowledge-seeking VQA.}
OK-VQA, A-OKVQA and InfoSeek pose questions whose answers require knowledge
beyond the image; MMSearch and related suites evaluate multimodal search
engines. All are static: once released, they enter pretraining corpora and
lose their ability to measure search. \todo{cite}

\paragraph{Freshness-oriented benchmarks.}
FreshQA (text-only) and Dyn-VQA target questions whose answers change over
time, but rely on manual curation and infrequent refreshes. LiveVQA
constructs VQA from recent news, and LiveBench refreshes text tasks monthly;
neither refreshes daily, ships gold evidence for oracle evaluation, nor
attributes search failures. \bench{} differs on all three axes.

\section{The \bench{} Benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\subsection{Design goals}
(i) \emph{Contamination-free by construction}: answers must postdate any
model's training cutoff, enforced by a 48-hour freshness window rather than
by trust; (ii) \emph{search-necessary}: closed-book accuracy $\approx$
chance, enforced by filtering; (iii) \emph{verifiable}: every item ships
evidence and provenance; (iv) \emph{sustainable}: fully automatic and cheap
enough to run daily forever.

\subsection{Construction pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}

\paragraph{Stage 0: fresh-news crawling.}
We poll 20+ RSS channels (Chinese and English; technology, world news,
politics, sports, finance, entertainment) and retain items published within
48 hours, enforcing freshness from RSS metadata or dates embedded in article
URLs. For each article we extract main text and the lead news image
(\texttt{og:image} plus per-site content-region heuristics), discarding
articles with short text ($<$300 chars) or small/decorative images. On
2026-07-14 this yielded 292 usable (image, article) pairs from $\sim$550
candidates.

\paragraph{Stage A: question generation.}
A VLM (Doubao Seed 2.0 Pro) receives the image and article and writes up to
three English MCQs under constraints: the question must be grounded in the
image (the image is needed to identify the entity/event under discussion);
the answer must come from the article's fresh content, not from the image or
world knowledge; the answer must not be readable as text in the image; the
four options must be mutually exclusive and of matched type and granularity;
each item must include a verbatim evidence quote. Questions are typed into
six categories (identification, factual detail, numerical, temporal, causal,
cross-modal).

\paragraph{Stage B: closed-book anti-memorization filter.}
The VLM answers each candidate given only the image and options. Items
answered correctly are discarded: they are solvable from parametric knowledge
or guessable from option design, and would contaminate the search
measurement. In the pilot, this removed \textbf{58.4\%} (292/500) of fluent,
well-formed candidates---a striking measurement of how much ``fresh'' news
content is in fact predictable---and simultaneously bounds MCQ guessability.

\paragraph{Stage C: oracle answerability filter.}
The VLM answers again with the gold evidence snippet appended. Items it still
fails are discarded as malformed or under-specified (1.4\% in the pilot).
Retained items are thus certified end-to-end: \emph{a model that retrieves
the evidence can answer; a model that cannot search cannot}.

\paragraph{Debiasing.}
Option positions are reshuffled post hoc to remove generator position bias
(the raw generator placed 45\% of correct answers at position B; after
reshuffling the distribution is near-uniform).

\section{Diagnostic Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Let $q$ be a question with gold answer $a^\ast$ and evidence $e^\ast$. Each
agent is evaluated under three settings:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Closed-book} $\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{cb}}$: image + question
  only. By construction $\approx$ chance for the filter model; deviations for
  other models measure knowledge overlap and option-prior exploitation.
  \item \textbf{With-search} $\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{ws}}$: the agent may
  issue web queries and read results. Headline score.
  \item \textbf{Oracle-evidence} $\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{or}}$: $e^\ast$ (and
  the source title) is provided. Upper bound; isolates grounding ability.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Error decomposition.}
For items failed under with-search but solved under oracle, we inspect the
agent's retrieval trace $R(q)$ (retrieved pages/snippets):
\begin{align*}
\text{retrieval failure:} &\quad e^\ast \notin R(q), \\
\text{distraction failure:} &\quad e^\ast \in R(q)\ \text{yet}\ \hat a \neq a^\ast, \\
\text{utilization failure:} &\quad \hat a \neq a^\ast\ \text{under oracle}.
\end{align*}
Evidence membership $e^\ast \in R(q)$ is checked by fuzzy matching the
evidence string (and its translation) against retrieved content, with a VLM
adjudicator for borderline cases. \todo{describe matcher validation}

\paragraph{Distraction stress test.}
We additionally re-run the oracle setting with $k \in \{0,2,4,8,16\}$
injected hard negatives---passages from same-day articles on related topics
---to chart $\mathrm{Acc}(k)$, a direct measurement of the ``lost in
retrieved context'' effect.

\section{Limitations}
Topic skew follows the RSS mix (tech-heavy in the pilot; mitigated by
stratified sampling as channels are added). MCQ format bounds but does not
eliminate guessing; an open-ended track using the same items is natural
future work. The generator-as-filter design could in principle bias item
difficulty toward the filter model's blind spots; the cross-generator
experiment and human audit quantify this risk.

\section{Ethics Statement}
All content is publicly published news; we store snippets and links with
full provenance and respect robots.txt. Images are redistributed at reduced
resolution for research use; takedown requests are honored. The benchmark
measures--and thus may accelerate--agents' ability to consume news; we
release with a research-only license.
